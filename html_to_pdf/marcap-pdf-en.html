Detailed Manufacturer Analysis

Why the leaders moved

Rank #1

JinkoSolar

Strategy: Early and aggressive adoption of N-type TOPCon technology.

Key factor: Leveraged a large Southeast Asia manufacturing base to maintain U.S. access while dominating China and Europe.

Milestone: Became the first company to cross the 200 GW cumulative shipment mark.

Rank #2

LONGi Solar

Strategy: A mid-period shift from TOPCon to BC Back Contact technology.

Key factor: Trailed Jinko in 2024 during transition, then 2025 volumes surged as HPBC 2.0 lines reached full capacity.

Status: Remains the world's most valuable solar company by market capitalization.

Rank #3

Trina Solar

Strategy: Championing the 210mm large-wafer standard.

Key factor: Highly successful in Middle East and Latin America utility-scale markets.

Innovation: Integrated Trina Storage into module sales, increasing contract value.

Rank #4

JA Solar

Strategy: Balanced geographic distribution and conservative financial management.

Key factor: Maintained consistent vertical integration, producing nearly 80% of components internally.

Advantage: Supply-chain control helped shield the company against price volatility.

Rank #5

Tongwei Solar

Strategy: Moving from cell supplier to module brand.

Key factor: Used polysilicon and cell dominance to undercut competitors on finished module pricing.

Growth: Highest year-over-year growth rate among the top five.

Critical Market Observations

  • Price erosion: Module prices hit record lows under $0.10/watt in some regions, forcing smaller Tier 2 manufacturers out of the market.
  • N-type dominance: By the end of 2025, N-type modules including TOPCon, HJT, and BC became the industry standard, making up over 85% of these shipments.
  • The gap: A significant volume gap remains between JA Solar at #4 and Tongwei at #5, separating the established Big Four from the rest of the industry.
